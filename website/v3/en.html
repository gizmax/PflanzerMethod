Pflanzer Method is an open source method and tool for AI development in companies. It seats business, product, design, development, security and operations in shared sessions. In a three-hour Build, AI produces three running variants. You decide on Go, Iterate or Kill within a week, and the winner goes to development as a pull request, with sign-offs.

Vibe coding has hit the edge of the silo.Vibe coding speeds a company up only when everyone sits at the table. That is why Pflanzer Method exists.

We have watched companies try vibe coding to speed up slow processes. A team builds a prototype with AI in a few hours. Then security, legal and operations see it for the first time, and the questions start: where is the data, who will run it, did it pass review? Questions that could have been settled on day one arrive weeks later, and the prototype waits in a queue.

AI sped up writing code. The handoffs between departments stayed the same. People who start with vibe coding hit the edge of their silo. Their work goes no further, so the speedup never happens.

Pflanzer Method breaks the silos by putting representatives of all six steps of the path into shared sessions from day one. Security and operations do not judge a finished product at the end. They sign off along the way, in short 20-minute windows.

How Pflanzer Method breaks the silos

Six steps of the path, each with a named person. Underneath, the tool tracks sign-offs, quality checks, the handoff to development and measurement after release.

Today

Handoffs in a chain

Business hands over to product, product to development, development to security. Security, legal and operations see the prototype for the first time at the end, and every handoff adds interpretation and a late veto.

Kickoff, 15 min

Everyone at one table

Representatives of the six steps of the path sit in shared sessions from day one. The core is 3–4 people; the others join for 20-minute windows. /pm start

Build, 3 h

Three running variants

AI builds variants A, B and C in your repository. The core sits at one screen. /pm build

Feedback round, 3–5 days

Representatives click and write

One address, password sent separately. Everyone writes what works and what doesn't, right on the screen. pflanzer share

Decision, 60–90 min

Decisions are made on running code

The Decider says Go, Iterate or Kill and votes last. Kill is a valid outcome. /pm decide

Ship, 1–2 days

To production, signed off

The winner passes the automated checks. The brief, security and operations are signed off along the way, and their conditions are in the PR text. After 30 days, the retro measures how much code stayed in production. /pm ship

Three rules that do not bend

Simplicity on the outside rests on a few fixed points inside.

AI prepares, a human signs

AI can stand in for a missing role with a draft. The Decider and the developer are always human, and AI never overrides their sign-off.

Decisions are made on running code

Three working variants in your repository instead of a document or mockup.

Kill is a valid outcome

If a variant misses the metric, the project ends with a lesson. The lesson goes into the overview of all projects.

The worst outcome is a Kill after one week

If the idea does not work, you find out on running code, before development starts. The project ends with a lesson, and the lesson goes into the overview of all projects. Kill is a valid outcome in the method, and the team learns from it.

Who the method is for, and when not

The method suits new features and internal tools that can be built and shown in an afternoon.

It fits when

  • you have a Decider with a mandate to say both Go and Kill,
  • the team has at least one developer,
  • the feature can be built in your repository and shown in a browser,
  • you are trying vibe coding and nothing got faster.

Don't use it for

  • payments and payment flows,
  • systems under AI Act high-risk or DORA without a governance layer,
  • native mobile apps with a store release cycle,
  • projects with a strong external dependency you cannot simulate.

Common questions

Answers to what teams ask before their first pilot.

How is it different from a design sprint or a hackathon?

The output is running code in your repository, and security, legal and operations sit in the shared sessions too. The tool handles window sign-offs, automated checks and the handoff of the winning variant to development as a pull request.

Do we have to use Claude Code?

No. The pflanzer command works from the terminal, from pflanzer start through to Ship. Claude Code adds a single entry command, /pm, and the AI builder.

What if we have no security person, designer or ops person?

AI can stand in for a missing role with a draft. A human always signs the window, and an AI draft never overrides that signature. The Decider and the developer are always human.

What if security says no anyway?

Then it says so in a window along the way, not weeks later over a finished product. The conditions from the sign-off are written into the PR text. Only the Decider can override a missing sign-off, knowingly and with a reason in the decision log.

Do we have to change how we develop?

No. The winner goes into your repository as a pull request and on through your normal pipeline. The method changes who sits in on the decision and when, not your development process.

How much time does a pilot take?

Kickoff takes 15 minutes, Build 3 hours, the feedback round 3–5 days, the Decision 60–90 minutes. A pilot comes to roughly 6–8 person-days, a demo to 5, a live rollout to 14.

Do you have results from real companies?

Not yet. The method has gone through 28 trial runs of the full path on fictional projects. From the first pilot, the tool measures days from Go to merge and the share of code that survived in production. We will publish those numbers once we have them.

How much does the method cost?

The method and the tool are open source under the MIT license. You pay for people's time and the AI you use. The tool shows the AI cost in its report.
